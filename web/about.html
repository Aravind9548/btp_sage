01What the Project Does

It contributes Path D: run fully choreographed while nothing is wrong, and only when a step fails elect a coordinator on the spot — which then schedules compensations against the dependency graph instead of walking them backwards one at a time. You keep choreography's decoupling and most of its speed, and you get orchestration's ordering guarantee.

The claim is backed by 1,500+ executions over four sweeps with deterministic fault injection. Importantly, the correctness verifier is a separate component that reads only the event log. It never sees what a scheduler intended, only what provably happened and in what order — so a scheduler cannot mark its own homework.

02What Is Genuinely Newthree things
01Zero idle cost
Nothing to pay when nothing is wrong

No coordinator process, no lease checks, no heartbeat on the happy path — it is ordinary choreography. The election machinery only wakes up on failure, which is the one moment a coordinator earns its keep.

02Ablated claim
The scheduler is the win, and I proved it

The elected leader reads the compensation graph and fires independent steps together. The ablation holds the election fixed and swaps only dispatch order — LIFO comes out 194% slower at width 8. The speed comes from the graph, not from electing someone.

03Cloud validated
It survives contact with AWS

The lease race maps onto a DynamoDB conditional write, the state onto string sets, the bus onto SNS/SQS or Kafka. Same coordination code, zero edits. Crash recovery falls out of lease TTL plus idempotent re-election.

04The One-Sentence Version

The literature asks you to choose between orchestration and choreography. That choice is false, because the coordinator is only needed during a rollback — so elect one then, and not before.

Doing it that way gets you all three at once:

  • choreography's decoupling — nothing is a permanent coordinator
  • orchestration's ordering guarantee — 100% correctness, verified from the log
  • choreography's speed profile — parallel rollback, flat as the graph widens

And the ablation tells you which half of the idea is load-bearing: swap graph-aware dispatch for LIFO and you keep correctness but lose all the speed, landing right back on orchestration's linear curve. The election is necessary; the scheduler is what makes it worth doing.
