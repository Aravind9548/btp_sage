Audit

Trust the numbers, not the pitch

Two measurement bugs this benchmark caught in its own plumbing — both timing artifacts, neither one touching a correctness result. The harness was audited, not just run.

Two findings

1

EventBus contention inflated Path B by 6×

An earlier version shared one EventBus instance — and its fixed 32-worker thread pool — across every concurrently-running saga in a sweep. Since EventBus topics are already saga_id-scoped, sharing the object bought nothing, but its bounded pool became a point of contention: hundreds of concurrent Path B sagas queued behind each other for the same 32 threads.

Caught by noticing Path B's numbers moved when the outer sweep's unrelated concurrency setting changed, despite Path B's own logic being untouched — a signal that some resource was shared across unrelated runs. Fixed with a fresh EventBus per saga.

~800ms → ~5,270ms, purely from benchmark plumbing, not from choreography itself
2

200 worker threads on 12 cores was quietly measuring contention, not coordination

run_experiments.py ran every sweep against one ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=200) on a 12-logical-core machine. With Path D's nested election/dispatch threads on top, peak concurrent OS threads reached the high hundreds — roughly 15–20× oversubscribed.

Not just noise: DAG width 8's mean makespan came out ~1,800ms against a ~950–1,070ms baseline at every other width in the same run — a real regime shift — and the coordination-overhead-ratio metric hit individual spikes as high as 54%.

Dropping to 48 workers erased the width-8 outlier and cut overhead to consistently under 1.5%, at unchanged wall time (~130s either way) — proof the extra threads bought nothing but contention.

Why B's 0% survives scrutiny

It would be a mistake to present "choreography violated ordering in 100% of trials" as an empirical rate — with fixed, non-random configuration, that's arithmetic, not measurement. The interesting question is why it's deterministic.

The first-pass guess — "Notification's 100ms compensation is simply shorter than Payment's 800ms" — turned out to be wrong, not just informal. Notification's own duration never appears in the check at all; only when it starts matters. Sweeping it from 1ms to 3,200ms changed nothing: 0% at every value.

Sweeping Payment's duration instead — down to 0ms — also stayed at 0%. The real cause: EventBus dispatches subscribers in subscription order, which follows forward execution order, so Notification's reactor always starts fractionally before Payment's. Given that, Payment's finish can never precede its own start, which already follows Notification's start — logically guaranteed, not merely likely. Reversing dispatch order did surface a genuine crossover, but only inside a sub-millisecond band with no relationship to real service durations.
